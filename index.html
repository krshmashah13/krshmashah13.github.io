03.2Policy debates

Spending vs outcomes: India's health budget in context

Does spending more on health per person buy longer lives? This policy debate compares India with five other countries (China, Brazil, Indonesia, Pakistan and Bangladesh) using World Bank data from 2000 to 2023.

03.2.1Government health spending per person

India's government health spending per person rose from about $3.8 in 2000 to $33.0 in 2023, but it is still a fraction of China's ($436) and Brazil's ($446). The chart uses a log scale, so each gridline is ten times the one below.

How I made this: I downloaded World Bank data on government health spending and life expectancy and cleaned it in Python in Google Colab. I picked six countries, reshaped the tables from wide to long and fixed the dates. I saved the clean data as a CSV in my GitHub repository, then built the chart with Altair and saved it as a Vega-Lite JSON file.

03.2.2Life expectancy at birth

Bangladesh's life expectancy rose by about 13 years between 2000 and 2023, from 62.0 to 74.7. It passed India in 2003 and has stayed ahead since, even though Bangladesh spends far less government money per person ($7.7 against India's $33.0 in 2023). India gained about 9 years, from 62.7 to 72.0.

How I made this: the life expectancy data came from the same World Bank download as the spending data. I cleaned it in the same Google Colab notebook and saved it as a CSV in my GitHub repository. Then I built the chart with Altair, using the same colours as the spending chart, and saved it as a Vega-Lite JSON file.

03.2.3Spending against life expectancy, 2023

In 2023, Bangladesh had a higher life expectancy than India (74.7 against 72.0 years) on less than a quarter of India's government health spending per person ($7.7 against $33.0). China and Brazil spend about 13 times India's amount and live four to six years longer, while Pakistan, spending about a third as much as India, has the lowest life expectancy at 67.6.

How I made this: I used the merged spending and life expectancy table from my Colab notebook, saved as a CSV in my GitHub repository. In Altair I kept the 2023 rows, plotted one dot per country on a log scale, made India's dot larger, and saved the chart as a Vega-Lite JSON file.

Notes: These figures cover government spending only. Private spending can be a large share of the total in some of these countries, so total health spending may look very different. Life expectancy depends on many things besides health spending, so these charts show association, not cause.

Sources: Government spending: WHO Global Health Expenditure Database, via the World Bank (SH.XPD.GHED.PC.CD). Life expectancy at birth: World Development Indicators, World Bank (SP.DYN.LE00.IN).

05.Accessing data

05.1The API chart

This chart is drawn from live World Bank data each time the page loads. India's life expectancy fell by almost three years in 2021, from 70.2 to 67.3, before recovering to 72.0 in 2023. Every other country dipped too, except China, whose life expectancy barely moved.

The data comes from the World Bank Data360 API. The base URL is https://data360api.worldbank.org/data360/data, and the request asks for the database WB_WDI (World Development Indicators), the indicator WB_WDI_SP_DYN_LE00_IN (life expectancy at birth), six countries (IND, CHN, BRA, IDN, PAK, BGD) and the years 2000 to 2023.

Full URL: https://data360api.worldbank.org/data360/data?DATABASE_ID=WB_WDI&INDICATOR=WB_WDI_SP_DYN_LE00_IN&REF_AREA=IND,CHN,BRA,IDN,PAK,BGD&timePeriodFrom=2000&timePeriodTo=2023

How I made this: I opened the address in my browser to check the data first. It comes back as JSON in a tidy format, with one row per country per year (144 rows), but the values are stored as text, the countries as codes and the years as plain numbers. In the chart spec I turned the values into numbers, the codes into country names and the years into dates. The chart reads the API directly, so it updates if the World Bank revises its figures.

05.2The scraped chart

Bangladesh and Pakistan spend less on health per person than India: $179 each against India's $273 in 2022. India is still not the lowest spender anywhere, because 46 of the 192 countries on the list spend less.

How I made this: Wikipedia's list of countries by total health expenditure per capita has no data download, so I scraped the page in Google Colab. I downloaded it with Python's requests package and pulled out the second table (WHO data for 192 countries) with pandas. I renamed the columns, kept only the numbers, because the table can include commas and footnote marks, and saved the result as a CSV in my GitHub repository. The chart reads that file and shows my six countries.

Note: these WHO figures for 2022 are not the same as the OECD figures in 04.3, which come from a different source and year, so India is $273 here and $312 there.
